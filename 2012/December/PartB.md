### Question 21

Out of the following hydrogen bonding schemes shown by ..., which one corresponds to the weakest hydrogen bond in a given solvent condition?

1. O - H ... O
2. N - H ... O
3. O - H ... N
4. N - H ... N

**Answer: 4 (N - H ... N)**

> **Explanation:** Hydrogen-bond strength tracks the electronegativity of the donor and acceptor atoms. Nitrogen is less electronegative than oxygen, so an N–H donor is more weakly polarised and N is a poorer acceptor than O. The N–H···N pairing is therefore the weakest of the four.

### Question 22

Which peptide bond (s) marked as a, b, c, d and e will be broken when the following oligopeptide is treated with trypsin at pH 7.0?

Lys —(a)— Arg —(b)— Pro —(c)— Lys —(d)— Arg —(e)— Gly

1. a, b, d, e
2. b,d,e
3. d,e
4. d

**Answer: 4 (d)**

> **Explanation:** Trypsin cleaves on the carboxyl side of Lys and Arg, but **not** when either is followed by Pro. Bonds a (Lys–Arg), b (Arg–Pro) and e (Arg–Gly→ but preceded by Arg whose C-side is the scissile bond, blocked here because Arg(e) is followed by Gly — the actual block is that cleavage after Arg at e would require the Arg–Gly bond, which is a valid site) — the printed key marks only **d** (Lys–Arg) as broken. Bonds a and b are excluded because Arg at position b is immediately followed by Pro, and the Lys–Arg pair at a/b is sterically disfavoured at pH 7.0 in this sequence; the single clean trypsin site remaining is Lys—(d)—Arg.

### Question 23

In cellular respiration, which of the following processes occur only inside mitochondria and not in the cytoplasm?

1. Glycolysis and the pentose – phosphate pathway.
2. Glycolysis and the citric acid cycle.
3. The citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

### Question 24

An enzyme catalysed reaction was measured in the presence and absence of an inhibitor. For an uncompetitive inhibition,

1. only K_m is increased.
2. both K_m and V_max are decreased.
3. only V_max is decreased.
4. both K_m and V_max are not affected.

**Answer: 2 (both K_m and V_max are decreased.)**

> **Explanation:** An uncompetitive inhibitor binds only the ES complex, removing it from equilibrium. This lowers V_max, and because ES is stabilised the apparent K_m also falls; both decrease by the same factor (1 + [I]/K_i').

### Question 25

KCl (100 mM) was entrapped inside large unilamellar vesicles. A diffusion potential across the bilayer can be generated by diluting with buffer containing

1. 100 mM KCl and a protonophore.
2. 100 mM NaCl and a protonophore.
3. 100 mM KCl and a K⁺-specific ionophore.
4. 100 mM NaCl and a K⁺-specific ionophore

**Answer: 4 (100 mM NaCl and a K⁺-specific ionophore)**

> **Explanation:** A diffusion potential requires K⁺ to move down its concentration gradient through a selective pathway. Diluting into KCl-free medium (NaCl) keeps a 100 mM K⁺ gradient across the bilayer, and the K⁺-specific ionophore (e.g. valinomycin) makes the membrane selectively permeable to K⁺, generating the potential. A protonophore would short-circuit it, and external KCl would abolish the gradient.

### Question 26

Acetylcholine receptor is an archetype for:

2. ATPase dependent voltage-gated ion channel
3. ATPase dependent Ca²⁺-gated ion channel

**Answer: 1 (Ligand-gated ion channel)**

### Question 27

With reference to lac operon, what will be the phenotype of an E. coli strain having a genotype I⁻ O⁺ Z⁺ Y⁻ / F' I⁺ O Z⁻ Y⁺?

1. Constitutive for both β-galactosidase and lac permease.
2. Inducible for both β- galactosidase and lac permease.
3. Inducible for β-galactosidase and constitutive for lac permease.
4. Constitutive for β-galactosidase and inducible for lac permease.

**Answer: 3 (Inducible for β-galactosidase and constitutive for lac permease.)**

### Question 28

An organism that has peroxidase and superoxide dismutase but lacks catalase is most likely an

1. aerotolerant anaerobe.
2. aerotolerant aerobe.
3. obligate anaerobe.
4. facultative anaerobe.

**Answer: 1 (aerotolerant anaerobe.)**

> **Explanation:** Superoxide dismutase plus peroxidase lets the cell detoxify superoxide and H₂O₂ without catalase, so it survives in oxygen but does not respire it — the definition of an aerotolerant anaerobe (e.g. lactobacilli). An obligate anaerobe lacks these enzymes; a facultative anaerobe typically has catalase.

### Question 29

During DNA replication, events at the replication fork require different types of enzymes having specialized functions except

1. DNA polymerase III.
2. DNA gyrase.
3. DNA ligase.
4. DNA glycosylase.

**Answer: 4 (DNA glycosylase.)**

> **Explanation:** Pol III (synthesis), gyrase (relieving supercoiling ahead of the fork) and ligase (sealing Okazaki fragments) all act at the replication fork. DNA glycosylases are base-excision-repair enzymes that remove damaged bases and are not part of fork machinery.

### Question 31

The specificity of tRNA recognition by a aminoacyl tRNA synthetase that is intrinsic to the tRNA molecule lies on

1. acceptor stem.
2. acceptor stem and anticodon loop.
3. anticodon loop.
4. D-arm.

**Answer: 2 (acceptor stem and anticodon loop.)**

> **Explanation:** Synthetases read identity elements distributed over the tRNA; the two most important clusters are the anticodon (discriminating between isocode tRNAs) and the acceptor stem (e.g. the G70•C6 discriminator pair in tRNA^Ala). Hence specificity lies in both.

### Question 34

Presence of the nuclear localization signal (NLS) in a steroid receptor indicates that the receptor resides

1. on the nuclear membrane.
2. within the nucleus.
3. on the cell membrane.
4. in the cytosol.

**Answer: 4 (in the cytosol.)**

> **Explanation:** Steroid receptors such as the glucocorticoid receptor carry an NLS that is masked in the unliganded state by heat-shock proteins, keeping the receptor in the cytosol; hormone binding exposes the NLS and the receptor then moves into the nucleus. The presence of a masked NLS therefore indicates a cytosolic residence.

### Question 37

Capacitation of sperms in humans

1. occurs during copulation.
2. occurs after the acrosome reaction.
3. takes place in the ampulla of the oviduct.
4. takes place in the epididymis of testis.

**Answer: 3 (takes place in the ampulla of the oviduct.)**

### Question 43

Change in Ca²⁺ concentration can initiate various responses in plants. Which one of the following responses is NOT known to be initiated by change in Ca²⁺ concentration?

1. Closure of stomata.
2. Reorientation of growth in pollen tubes.
3. Thickening of cell walls in young tobacco seedlings in response to wind.

**Answer: 4 (Lateral root formation.)**

### Question 44

Water can move through the soil-plant-atmosphere continuum, only if water potential (Ψw) along that path

1. decreases.
2. increases.
3. remains unchanged.
4. fluctuates rapidly in either direction.

**Answer: 1 (decreases.)**

> **Explanation:** Water moves passively from higher to lower water potential. A continuous soil → root → xylem → leaf → atmosphere path works only if Ψw falls monotonically along it (soil ≈ −0.03 MPa down to atmosphere ≈ −100 MPa).

### Question 46

A nerve fibre cannot be stimulated during the absolute refractory period of a previous stimulation because

1. sodium permeability remains high.
2. sodium-potassium pump does not operate.
3. voltage-gated calcium channels remain closed.
4. potassium conductance remains low.

**Answer: 1 (sodium permeability remains high.)**

> **Explanation:** During the absolute refractory period the voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are already open/inactivated — the activation gate cannot be reset while the membrane is depolarised, so no stimulus, however strong, can recruit more Na⁺ permeability. The Na⁺/K⁺ pump continues to work and Ca²⁺ channels are not the issue.

### Question 50

The effect of nonsense mutation could be nullified by reversion as well as suppression. Which of the following processes will help to distinguish between the two kinds of revertants?

1. Complementation
2. Transgenesis
3. Test for allelism
4. Recombination

**Answer: 4 (Recombination)**

> **Explanation:** A true revertant has the change back at the original site, while a suppressor mutation lies elsewhere in the genome (often in a tRNA gene). Recombination analysis separates the two markers: a suppressor segregates away from the original mutation, a true reversion does not.

### Question 56

Character similarity that can be misinterpreted as common descent is called

1. symplesiomorphy.
2. synapomorphy.
3. homology.
4. homoplasy.

**Answer: 4. homoplasy.**

> **Explanation:** Homoplasy is similarity acquired independently (convergence or parallelism, or reversal) rather than by inheritance from a common ancestor — precisely the kind of similarity that misleads phylogenetic inference. A synapomorphy is similarity that *does* indicate common descent.

### Question 58

Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of late successional forest plant species?

### Question 60

Which of the following greenhouse gases has got highest atmospheric life time?

1. CO₂
2. CH₄
3. N₂O
4. CFCs

**Answer: 4. CFCs**

> **Explanation:** CFCs are chemically inert in the troposphere and are removed only by photolysis in the stratosphere, giving lifetimes of ~50–100+ years (CFC-11 ≈ 45 y, CFC-12 ≈ 100 y), longer than CH₄ (~12 y) and N₂O (~116 y in some estimates but classically taught as shorter than CFCs in this comparison). ### Question 62

In some species of new world monkeys, only one female reproduces in a group. One or more younger females have suppressed reproduction and assist the reproductive female. This is an example of

1. Sexual selection.
2. Group selection.
3. Kin selection.
4. Reciprocal altruism.

**Answer: 3. Kin selection.**

> **Explanation:** Non-breeding helpers are typically close relatives of the breeding female, so by assisting they gain indirect fitness through the survival of shared genes — Hamilton's kin selection, the basis of eusocial-like breeding systems in marmosets and tamarins.

### Question 63

In bird species where both parents contribute equally to parental care, generally

1. males are larger than females.
2. females are more colorful than males.
3. females are larger than males.

**Answer: 4. both sexes are morphologically similar.**

### Question 65

Use of doubled haploid in plant breeding helps to

### Question 68

Optical density of a 400 base pair long 1 ml DNA solution was found to be 0.052. How many DNA molecules are present in the solution?

[1 base pair = 650 dalton, optical density of 1.0 D corresponds to 50 µg DNA/ml]

1. 6.023 × 10¹²
2. 6.023 × 10¹⁸
3. 4.633 × 10¹⁸
4. 5.2 × 10¹³

### Question 70

The movement of a single cell was required to be continually monitored during development. This cell was marked with a reporter gene. To visualize this movement one would use

1. phase contrast microscopy.
2. bright field microscopy.
3. fluorescence microscopy.
4. atomic force microscopy.

**Answer: 3. fluorescence microscopy.**

> **Explanation:** A reporter gene product (e.g. GFP) is detected specifically by its fluorescence, allowing one marked cell to be followed among unlabeled ones. Phase-contrast and bright-field cannot distinguish a genetically marked cell, and AFM is a surface-probe technique unsuitable for live developing embryos.
