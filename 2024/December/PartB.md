### Question 21

*(Part B, Question No. 1 / Question ID 703160, Marks 2.00)*

Which one of the following is the most significant factor that explains the evolution of iteroparity in animals?

1. Slower developmental rates over their lifespan
2. Predictable environmental conditions
3. Low adult survival rates
4. Cost per reproductive event is high

**Answer: 2 (Predictable environmental conditions)**

> **Explanation:** Iteroparity — repeated breeding over several seasons — pays only when the animal can expect to survive to breed again, which is the case where the environment is predictable and adult survival between breeding attempts is reliable. Low adult survival (3) and a high cost per reproductive event (4) both favour the opposite strategy, semelparity (one big reproductive effort). Slower development (1) is a correlated life-history trait, not the driving factor. NTA's final key for Question ID 703160 is option 2.

### Question 22

*(Part B, Question No. 2 / Question ID 703149, Marks 2.00)*

In a population with the ABO blood group system, if the frequency of the allele *I^A* is 0.3, the frequency of the allele *I^B* is 0.2, and the frequency of the allele *i* is 0.5, what would be the expected percentage of population with blood group A, considering that the population is under Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium?

1. 9%
2. 24%
3. 39%
4. 42%

**Answer: 3 (39%)**

> **Explanation:** Blood group A arises from genotypes *I^A I^A* and *I^A i*. With $p=0.3$ and $r=0.5$: $p^2 + 2pr = 0.09 + 2(0.3)(0.5) = 0.09 + 0.30 = 0.39$, i.e. **39%**.

### Question 24

*(Part B, Question No. 4 / Question ID 703130, Marks 2.00)*

Match the columns:

| | Column X — *E. coli* genes involved in replication | | Column Y — Functional eukaryotic orthologues |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. | DnaB | i. | Pola/ primase |
| B | DnaC | ii. | cdc6 |
| C. | β clamp | iii. | PCNA |
| D. | DnaG | iv. | MCM complex |

Which one of the following options represents all correct matches between Column X and Column Y?

### Question 25

*(Part B, Question No. 5 / Question ID 703139, Marks 2.00)*

The regeneration of a severed Axolotl limb is mediated by which one of the following cellular responses to injury?

1. Blastema formation
2. Transdifferentiation
3. Induced pluripotency
4. Stem cell dedifferentiation

**Answer: 1 (Blastema formation)**

> **Explanation:** After amputation, mature cells at the stump dedifferentiate, proliferate and accumulate beneath an apical epithelial cap to form a **blastema**, a mass of progenitor cells that then re-differentiates to rebuild the missing structures. ### Question 26

*(Part B, Question No. 6 / Question ID 703170, Marks 2.00)*

A transmembrane receptor protein (X) and a transmembrane protein bound to the actin cytoskeleton (Y) with fluorescent tags were expressed in a cell. A fluorescence recovery after photobleaching (FRAP) experiment was performed on these proteins. Which one of the following options represents the most likely outcome of this experiment?

1. Fluorescence of both proteins will be recovered at the same rate.
2. Fluorescence of X will be recovered later than Y.
3. Fluorescence of Y will be recovered later than X.
4. Being membrane proteins, no recovery of fluorescence is expected.

**Answer: 3 (Fluorescence of Y will be recovered later than X.)**

> **Explanation:** FRAP recovery depends on lateral diffusion of unbleached molecules into the bleached patch. The free receptor X diffuses rapidly, so its fluorescence recovers quickly and to a large extent. Protein Y is anchored to the actin cytoskeleton, which greatly restricts its mobility, so it recovers slowly (and often only partially) — i.e. **later than X**.

### Question 27

*(Part B, Question No. 7 / Question ID 703128, Marks 2.00)*

Dynein is a microtubule-dependent motor protein essential for cellular processes. Which one of the following statements related to dynein function is INCORRECT?

1. It helps in cilia and flagella beating.
2. It transports organelles in anterograde direction.
3. It participates in spindle assembly and positioning.
4. It interacts with dynactin for efficient transport.

**Answer: 2 (It transports organelles in anterograde direction.)**

> **Explanation:** Cytoplasmic dynein is the **minus-end-directed (retrograde)** motor: it carries cargo from the cell periphery toward the centre, and toward the spindle poles. The other statements are true — axonemal dynein powers cilia and flagella beating, dynein positions the spindle, and it needs dynactin for processive cargo binding.

### Question 28

*(Part B, Question No. 8 / Question ID 703166, Marks 2.00)*

Which of the following molecular marker techniques uses a single primer in a PCR reaction for identifying polymorphisms between genotypes?

1. SSR (Simple sequence repeats)
2. AFLP (Amplified fragment length polymorphism)
3. RAPD (Randomly amplified polymorphic DNA)
4. SCAR (Sequence characterized amplified regions)

**Answer: 3 (RAPD (Randomly amplified polymorphic DNA))**

> **Explanation:** RAPD-PCR amplifies random segments of genomic DNA with **one short arbitrary primer** (~10 nt) at low annealing temperature; the presence or absence of bands between genotypes reveals polymorphism. SSR and SCAR need primer pairs flanking a known site, and AFLP uses adapter-ligated primers in a two-primer (pre- and selective) scheme.

Which one of the following statements regarding cytoskeleton proteins is INCORRECT?

### Question 31

*(Part B, Question No. 11 / Question ID 703121, Marks 2.00)*

A novel organism synthesizes proteins using ribosomes from the C-terminal end to the N-terminal end rather than the usual direction (N- to C-terminus). Which main chain atom will be the nucleophile for the reaction to form peptide bonds?

1. Nitrogen of the amine group
2. Carbon of the carboxyl group
3. Oxygen of the carboxyl group
4. Alpha-carbon (Cα)

**Answer: 1 (Nitrogen of the amine group)**

> **Explanation:** Peptide-bond formation is always a nucleophilic attack of the **α-amino nitrogen** of the aminoacyl group in the A site on the ester carbonyl carbon of the peptidyl-tRNA in the P site. The chemistry of the peptidyl transferase centre does not depend on the direction in which the chain is being read, so even for a hypothetical C→N ribosome the attacking atom remains the amine nitrogen.

### Question 32

*(Part B, Question No. 12 / Question ID 703164, Marks 2.00)*

Many marine mammals communicate over several kilometres in the ocean. This is due to

1. their ability to communicate using ultrasound.
2. the higher density of water compared to air.
3. lower oxygen levels in the open ocean.
4. water filtering out high-frequency sounds.

**Answer: 2 (the higher density of water compared to air.)**

> **Explanation:** Sound travels through water with far less loss of intensity than through air because water is much denser and less compressible, so acoustic energy propagates over kilometres with little attenuation. Marine mammals use **infra**sound rather than ultrasound for long-range calls (ruling out 1), oxygen level is irrelevant (3), and it is high-frequency *absorption* that limits range — a reason for low frequencies, not for long-distance communication (4).

The CaM Kinase II acts as a molecular memory device as well as a frequency decoder of Ca²⁺ oscillation. Which one of the following statements regarding CaM Kinase II is INCORRECT?

### Question 38

*(Part B, Question No. 18 / Question ID 703163, Marks 2.00)*

Cleaner fish remove parasites from larger fish. Which evolutionary mechanism most likely maintains this interaction?

1. Kin selection
2. Mutualism
3. Direct competition
4. Character displacement

**Answer: 2 (Mutualism)**

> **Explanation:** Both partners gain — the cleaner gets food, the client gets parasites removed — so the interaction is a **mutualism**, maintained by by-product benefit and partner-choice/sanction mechanisms (a cleaner that bites the client is avoided). The fish are unrelated, so kin selection does not apply, and competition or character displacement describe antagonistic or divergence processes.

Which one of the following is generated in skeletal muscle cells by the single quanta of acetylcholine released from the motor nerve terminals?

1. Inhibitory post-synaptic potential
2. Inhibitory junction potential
3. Endplate potential
4. Miniature endplate potential

**Answer: 4 (Miniature endplate potential)**

> **Explanation:** A **quantum** is the content of one synaptic vesicle. Spontaneous release of a single vesicle's ACh depolarises the endplate by only ~0.4–1 mV, which is the **miniature endplate potential (mEPP)**. The full endplate potential (3) requires the synchronous release of many hundreds of quanta evoked by a nerve impulse.

*(Part B, Question No. 25 / Question ID 703150, Marks 2.00)*

UV mutagenesis was performed to isolate mutants of the *lacZ* gene. The mutation rate of this gene is 1 X 10⁻⁴ per cell division. Assuming that an *E. coli* culture was initiated from a culture density of 1 X 10² cells and grown to a density of 1 X 10⁶ cells, how many *lacZ* mutants are expected in this population?

1. ~ 1
2. ~ 10
3. ~ 100
4. ~ 1000

**Answer: 3 (~ 100)**

> **Explanation:** Going from 10² to 10⁶ cells means about 10⁶ new cells were produced, and each new cell corresponds to one division, so the number of division events is ≈10⁶. Glioblastomas, oligodendrogliomas, and astrocytomas harbour mutations in isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH). **Answer: 1 (CO₂ and Diethyl urea)**

The citric acid cycle (TCA) operates only in the presence of molecular oxygen (O₂). This is because

1. O₂ activates enzymatic dehydrogenation reactions in the cycle.
2. O₂ accepts electrons from electron transport chain, allowing reoxidation of NADH to NAD⁺.

**Answer: 2 (O₂ accepts electrons from electron transport chain, allowing reoxidation of NADH to NAD⁺.)**

### Question 57

*(Part B, Question No. 37 / Question ID 703129, Marks 2.00)*

Two types of mutant *E. coli* were identified: in the hypermethylation mutant (type A), DNA is methylated at the GATC sequences as soon as daughter DNA is synthesized; and in the second type, GATC sequences are never methylated (type B).

Which mutant will have a greater effect on the MMR system, leading to the accumulation of spontaneous mutations?

1. Type A > Type B
2. Type B > Type A
3. Type A = Type B
4. Type B mutants will not accumulate spontaneous mutations

**Answer: 1 (Type A > Type B)**

> **Explanation:** Mismatch repair uses the transient **hemimethylation** of newly replicated GATC sites to identify the daughter (unmethylated) strand and thereby the strand that carries the replication error. In the **hypermethylation** mutant (type A) the nascent strand is methylated immediately, so the strand-discrimination signal is destroyed from the outset and MMR cannot act at all — errors are fixed, giving a strong mutator phenotype. Hence type A > type B.

Tectonic uplift can alter the drainage patterns of rivers, thereby isolating populations of many species. Population isolation of this kind results from which one of the following processes?
