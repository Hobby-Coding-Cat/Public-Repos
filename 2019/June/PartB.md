### Question 21

Which one of the statements on protein conformation, detailed below is **INCORRECT**?

1. L-amino acids can occur in Type I' β-turns where φ, ψ are both positive.
2. A peptide rich in proline is unlikely to adopt α-helical structure.
3. Proline residues have high propensity to occur in β-turns.
4. The dihedral angles φ, ψ of amino acids in unfolded proteins are exclusively positive.

**Answer: 4 (The dihedral angles φ, ψ of amino acids in unfolded proteins are exclusively positive.)**

> **Explanation:** An unfolded (denatured) chain samples a broad region of Ramachandran space, so its φ and ψ values take on both positive and negative values — they are certainly not "exclusively positive". That makes 4 the incorrect statement. The others are true: Type I' turns do use positive φ,ψ in the strained positions (1); proline cannot donate an NH hydrogen bond and its ring clashes with the helix, so proline-rich peptides resist α-helices (2); and proline is strongly favoured at i+1 of β-turns (3).

### Question 22

Choose the **INCORRECT** statement from the following statements made for an enzyme-catalyzed reaction

1. The kinetic properties of allosteric enzymes do not diverge from Michaelis-Menten behaviour.
2. In feedback inhibition, the product of a pathway inhibits an enzyme of the pathway.
3. An antibody that binds tightly to the analog of the transition state intermediate of the reaction S→P, would promote formation of P when the analog is added to the reaction.
4. An enzyme with Kcat = 1.4 ×10⁴ s⁻¹ and Km = 9×10⁻⁵ M has activity close to the diffusion controlled limit.

**Answer: 1 (The kinetic properties of allosteric enzymes do not diverge from Michaelis-Menten behaviour.)**

> **Explanation:** Allosteric enzymes characteristically give **sigmoidal**, not hyperbolic, saturation curves, with cooperativity and T/R state transitions — so they *do* diverge from Michaelis-Menten behaviour, making statement 1 incorrect. Feedback inhibition by an end product (2) and transition-state-analog antibodies acting as catalysts (3) are standard facts, and Kcat/Km = 1.4×10⁴/9×10⁻⁵ ≈ 1.6×10⁸ M⁻¹s⁻¹ (4) is indeed at the diffusion-controlled limit.

### Question 25

The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump is found on the plasma membrane of most animal cells. A mutation in the intrinsic phosphorylation site of the pump is most likely to affect

1. the outward movement of Na⁺ only.
2. inward movement of K⁺ only.
3. both the inward and outward movement of K⁺ and Na⁺.
4. has no effect on pump activity but affects its stability.

**Answer: 3 (both the inward and outward movement of K⁺ and Na⁺.)**

### Question 27

To prepare individual tissue cells from a primary culture, the cell-cell and cell-matrix interaction must be broken. To achieve this, one would **NOT** use:

1. EDTA
2. Trypsin

4. Separase

### Question 28

Which one of the following statements is **NOT** true about nucleosomal organization of core particle?

1. The typical structure of DNA is altered in the middle of the core particle.
2. In core particle, DNA is organized as flat super helix with 1.65 turns around the histone octamer.
3. While forming 30 nm fibers, generally 6 nucleosomes per turn organize into a two-start helix.
4. The N-terminal histone tails in a core particle are strictly ordered and exit from the nucleosomes between turns of the DNA.

**Answer: 4 (The N-terminal histone tails in a core particle are strictly ordered and exit from the nucleosomes between turns of the DNA.)**

> **Explanation:** The N-terminal histone tails are intrinsically **disordered/flexible** — they are largely unresolved in nucleosome crystal structures and they project out to make inter-nucleosomal contacts and post-translational modifications. Describing them as "strictly ordered" is false. Statements 1–3 correctly describe the distorted DNA at the entry/exit and dyad region, ~1.65 left-handed turns of ~147 bp, and the two-start, ~6-per-turn 30 nm fibre.

### Question 29

During replication, RNaseH removes all of the RNA primer except the ribonucleotide directly linked to the DNA end. This is because

1. it can degrade RNA and DNA from their 5' end.
2. it can only cleave bonds between two ribonucleotides.
3. it can degrade RNA and DNA from their 3' end.
4. activity of RNaseH is inhibited by the presence of duplex containing both strands as DNA.

**Answer: 2 (it can only cleave bonds between two ribonucleotides.)**

> **Explanation:** RNase H removes RNA only from an RNA:DNA hybrid, and it cleaves the phosphodiester bond **between adjacent ribonucleotides**. The single terminal ribonucleotide joined to a deoxyribonucleotide therefore presents an RNA–DNA bond that RNase H cannot cut; that last residue is removed later by the 5'→3' exonuclease of DNA polymerase I (or by FEN1 in eukaryotes).

### Question 30

In a human cell line, a large fraction of double-strand DNA breaks are repaired by non-homologous end joining (NHEJ). An inhibitor of FLAP endonuclease will affect

1. recruitment of DNA-dependent kinase.
2. gap trimming.
3. DNA unwinding.
4. pairing of micro-homology regions.

**Answer: 2 (gap trimming.)**

> **Explanation:** In NHEJ, Artemis opens hairpins and the **FLAP endonuclease** (part of the Pol X4 / FEN1-type activity) performs the 5'-end "flap" trimming that removes single-stranded overhangs so the ends can be ligated. Inhibiting FLAP therefore blocks **gap/end trimming**, while DNA-PK recruitment (1), helicase unwinding (3) and microhomology pairing (4) are carried out by other components.

### Question 31

Sugar puckering in double stranded nucleic acids is exclusively

1. C-2' endo in double stranded DNA
2. C-3' endo in double stranded DNA
3. C-2' endo in double stranded RNA
4. C-3' endo in hybrid duplex with one strand as DNA and the other as RNA

**Answer: 4 (C-3' endo in hybrid duplex with one strand as DNA and the other as RNA)**

### Question 32

Eukaryotic mRNAs are modified to possess a 5' cap structure. Which one of the following is an **INCORRECT** statement about the function of the 5' cap structure?

2. It facilitates splicing of the nascent transcripts.

### Question 38

In *Xenopus* embryos, β-catenin plays an important role in the Dorsal/Ventral axis development. What would you expect if the endogenous glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) is knocked out by a dominant-negative form of GSK3 in the ventral cells of the early embryo?

1. Blocking of GSK3 on the ventral side has no effect. A normal embryo will form.
2. The resulting embryo will only have ventral sides.
3. A second axis will form.
4. The dorsal fate is suppressed.

**Answer: 3 (A second axis will form.)**

> **Explanation:** GSK3 normally phosphorylates β-catenin on the ventral side and targets it for degradation. A dominant-negative GSK3 stabilises β-catenin **ventrally**, so the ventral cells now read the dorsal signal (Siamois/Goosecoid activation) and organise a **secondary dorsal axis** — the classic Spemann-type twin induction. Suppressing dorsal fate (4) or an all-ventral embryo (2) are the opposite of what happens.

### Question 47

Which one is the correct sequence of events that takes place during phototransduction when light falls onto the retina?

1. Closure of Na⁺ channels→activation of transducin→decreased release of glutamate→ decrease in intracellular cGMP→structural changes in rhodopsin
2. Decreased release of glutamate→structural changes in rhodopsin→activation of transducin→ decrease in intracellular cGMP→closure of Na⁺ channels

### Question 48

A Lod score of 3 represents a Recombination Frequency (RF) that is

1. 3 times as likely as the hypothesis of no linkage.
2. 30 times as likely as the hypothesis of no linkage.
3. 100 times as likely as the hypothesis of no linkage.
4. 1000 times as likely as the hypothesis of no linkage.

**Answer: 4 (1000 times as likely as the hypothesis of no linkage.)**

> **Explanation:** The LOD score is log₁₀ of the odds ratio: LOD = log₁₀[L(θ)/L(θ = 0.5)]. A LOD of 3 therefore means 10³ = **1000 times** as likely as independent assortment — which is the classical threshold for declaring linkage.

### Question 49

Assuming that the A, B, C and D genes are not linked, the probability of a progeny being *AaBBccDd* from a cross between *AABbccDd* and *aaBBccDD* parents will be

1. 4/32
2. 3/16
3. 1/4
4. 3/32

**Answer: 3 (1/4)**

> **Explanation:** Score each locus independently for the progeny *AaBBccDd* from *AABbccDd* × *aaBBccDD*. **A:** AA × aa → every offspring is Aa, so P(Aa) = 1. **B:** Bb × BB → ½ BB : ½ Bb, so P(BB) = 1/2. **C:** cc × cc → all cc, so P(cc) = 1. **D:** Dd × DD → ½ DD : ½ Dd, so P(Dd) = 1/2. Multiplying the independent probabilities: 1 × 1/2 × 1 × 1/2 = **1/4**.

### Question 51

Which one of the following DNA markers can be used to distinguish between a homozygote and a heterozygote?

1. RAPD
2. AFLP
3. RFLP
4. ISSR

**Answer: 3 (RFLP)**

> **Explanation:** **RFLP** markers are **co-dominant**: a heterozygote shows *both* allele bands (the two fragment sizes), whereas a homozygote shows only one, so the two are directly distinguishable on a gel. RAPD (1) and ISSR (4) are strictly dominant — a heterozygote is indistinguishable from a homozygous band-present individual — and AFLP (2) as scored is likewise dominant (presence/absence of a fragment).

### Question 54

Depicted below is a phylogenetic tree of selected taxa:

![A phylogenetic tree of seven taxa A to G. Taxa A, B and C form Group X, enclosed in a dashed rounded box; taxa D, E, F and G form Group Y, enclosed in a second dashed rounded box. ### Question 56

A road is constructed through a wet tropical forest, following which the population of a species of forest butterfly declines. Which of the following is **NOT** a possible explanation for the road causing a decline in the forest butterfly population?

1. Road facilitates immigration of gap-loving species which compete with the forest species.
2. Road facilitates increased movement of individuals of the forest butterfly within the forest, which reduces genetic diversity.

### Question 57

Which one of the following statements regarding normal distribution is **NOT** correct?

1. It is symmetric around the mean.
2. It is symmetric around the median.
3. It is symmetric around the variance.
4. It is symmetric around the mode.

**Answer: 3 (It is symmetric around the variance.)**

3. greater biological competition in the tropics leads to narrower niches.
4. lower predation intensity in the tropics allows survival of more prey species.

**Answer: 4 (lower predation intensity in the tropics allows survival of more prey species.)**

> **Explanation:** The standard explanations for the latitudinal diversity gradient are the greater evolutionary time and climatic stability of the tropics (1), high habitat/spatial heterogeneity (2), and stronger biotic interactions producing niche specialisation (3). Predation intensity in the tropics is generally **higher**, not lower, and higher predation is invoked as a diversity-*promoting* force (keystone predation, reduced competitive exclusion) — so statement 4, which claims low tropical predation, is the exception.

Which one of the following statements is correct?

1. Ectomycorrhizal associations predominantly reduce phosphorus limitation, and endomycorrhizal associations reduce both nitrogen and phosphorus limitation.
2. Endomycorrhizal associations predominantly reduce phosphorus limitation, and ectomycorrhizal associations reduce both nitrogen and phosphorus limitation.
3. Ecto-and endo-mycorrhizal associations do not reduce nitrogen and phosphorus limitation.

**Answer: 2 (Endomycorrhizal associations predominantly reduce phosphorus limitation, and ectomycorrhizal associations reduce both nitrogen and phosphorus limitation.)**

### Question 60

Which one of the following statements is correct for the process of speciation?

1. Allopatric speciation occurs between adjacent populations.
2. Parapatric speciation may occur between geographically separated populations.
3. Sympatric speciation occurs within one continuously distributed population.
4. Sympatric speciation occurs when continuously distributed populations are fragmented.

**Answer: 3 (Sympatric speciation occurs within one continuously distributed population.)**

> **Explanation:** Sympatric speciation takes place **without geographic isolation**, within a single panmictic/continuously distributed population (e.g. by polyploidy, host-shifting or assortative mating). Allopatric speciation requires *separated* (not adjacent) populations (1); parapatric speciation needs contiguous populations with a hybrid zone, not geographically separated ones (2); and fragmentation describes allopatry, not sympatry (4).

### Question 61

Which one of the following does **NOT** contribute to microevolutionary change?

1. Mutation
2. Random mating
3. Genetic drift
4. Natural selection

**Answer: 2 (Random mating)**

> **Explanation:** Microevolution is a change in allele/genotype frequency, driven by mutation, migration, genetic drift and natural selection. **Random mating** by itself leaves allele and genotype frequencies unchanged (Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium); it is one of the assumptions of "no evolution", not a mechanism of change.

### Question 62

According to Hamilton's rule, altruistic behaviour can evolve when rb > c, where b is the extra benefit gained by the recipient as a result of the altruistic act, c is the cost to the actor arising from performing the altruistic act and r is the relatedness between the:

2. individual performing the altruistic act and the recipient
3. recipient and the offspring of the individual performing the altruistic act
4. individual performing the altruistic act and the members of its population

### Question 63

Analyses of nucleotide sequences of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) are particularly useful for evolutionary studies of living organisms because of the following reasons **EXCEPT**

2. no free-living organism lacks rRNA.

**Answer: 3 (rRNA, since critical for translation, can undergo lateral transfer amongst distant species.)**

2. Introducing a single gene for resistance to a particular pest in different genotypes.
3. Introducing different genes for resistance to a single pest in a single genotype.
4. Introducing a single gene for resistance to multiple pests in different genotypes.

**Answer: 3 (Introducing different genes for resistance to a single pest in a single genotype.)**

> **Explanation:** Gene pyramiding is the deliberate stacking of **several different resistance (R) genes against the same pest/pathogen into one genotype**, usually by successive backcrossing or marker-assisted selection, so that the pathogen cannot easily break resistance — hence durable resistance. ### Question 65

To test the impact of cAMP on protein kinase A conformation in cells, an investigator made FRET biosensor by fusing two fluorescent proteins at the N- and C-terminus of protein kinase A. In the absence of cAMP in the cellular milieu, no FRET signal was detected. However, upon cAMP addition, a strong emission at 530 nm was observed. What could be the best configuration of fluorophores that were used by the investigator?

1. Green fluorescent protein (GFP) and Red fluorescent protein (RFP).
2. CYAN fluorescent protein (CFP) and Yellow fluorescent protein (YFP).
3. Yellow fluorescent protein (YFP) and Red fluorescent protein (RFP).
4. Red fluorescent protein (RFP) and CYAN fluorescent protein (CFP).

**Answer: 2 (CYAN fluorescent protein (CFP) and Yellow fluorescent protein (YFP).)**

> **Explanation:** A FRET pair needs the **donor** to be excited and the **acceptor** to emit. CFP is the standard donor and **YFP the acceptor whose emission peaks at ~530 nm** — exactly the signal reported. In the closed (no-cAMP) conformation CFP and YFP are too far apart for FRET, so no 530 nm emission appears; cAMP-induced opening of the PKA biosensor brings the termini together and FRET is restored. The other pairs do not give a CFP-excited 530 nm acceptor signal (GFP/RFP and YFP/RFP emit at longer wavelengths, and RFP→CFP has the donor and acceptor the wrong way round for energy transfer).

### Question 66

In bioremediation by microorganisms detailed below, choose the **INCORRECT** option?

2. The bacteria do not get net energy by degrading contaminants.
3. Bacteria can produce oxidized or reduced species that can cause metals to precipitate.
4. Bacteria act on contaminants by aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

**Answer: 2 (The bacteria do not get net energy by degrading contaminants.)**

### Question 67

A multimeric protein when run on an SDS gel showed 2 bands at 20 kDa and 40 kDa. However, when the protein was run on a native gel, it showed a single band at 120 kDa. The native form of the protein would be

1. homotrimer.
2. heterotetramer.
3. heterodimer.
4. heterotrimer.

**Answer: 2 (heterotetramer.)**

> **Explanation:** SDS-PAGE resolves the protein into **two different subunits (20 and 40 kDa)**, so the complex must be *hetero*-meric — a homotrimer (1) is excluded because it would give a single band. The native mass is 120 kDa, and the stoichiometry that adds to it using both subunits is 40 + 40 + 20 + 20 = 120, i.e. **four subunits of two kinds = a heterotetramer**. A heterodimer (3, ≤60 kDa) and a heterotrimer (4, e.g. 40+40+20 = 100 or 40+20+20 = 80 kDa) cannot reach 120 kDa.

### Question 69

Orientation of a cloned DNA fragment (gene) in a plasmid vector can be checked by

1. PCR using two gene-specific primers.
2. Restriction digestion with an enzyme that has a single restriction site within the cloned gene and none in the vector.
3. PCR using a combination of one gene-specific primer and one vector-specific primer.
4. Restriction digestion with an enzyme that has two restriction sites within the vector sequence and none in the cloned gene.

**Answer: 3 (PCR using a combination of one gene-specific primer and one vector-specific primer.)**

> **Explanation:** An **insert–vector (colony) PCR** with one primer annealing in the vector flanking the cloning site and one inside the insert is orientation-specific: the product length (and whether one is obtained at all) differs depending on which way the insert went in, because the two vector primers flank the site asymmetrically. Two gene-specific primers (1) amplify regardless of orientation, and the single-site digestions (2, 4) cannot report direction.

### Question 70

The emission maximum of tryptophan fluorescence in a protein is ~ 335 nm. This suggests that tryptophan

1. is in a hydrophobic environment.
2. occurs in a helical segment.
3. has proximal cysteine residues.
4. is oxidized.

**Answer: 1 (is in a hydrophobic environment.)**

> **Explanation:** Tryptophan fluorescence is strongly environmentally sensitive: in a **non-polar (hydrophobic) buried pocket** the emission maximum is blue-shifted to ~330–335 nm, whereas in a polar/aqueous (surface-exposed) environment it shifts to ~350–355 nm. An emission at 335 nm therefore indicates the Trp sits in a hydrophobic environment. Helical context (2), nearby cysteine (3) or oxidation (4) are not what this wavelength reports.
