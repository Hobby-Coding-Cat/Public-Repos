### Question 21

Which one of the following non-covalent interactions between two non-bonded atoms A and B is most sensitive to the distance between them?

1. A and B are permanent dipoles and are involved in hydrogen bonding.
2. A and B are fully ionized and are involved in salt bridge formation.
3. A and B are uncharged and repel each other.
4. A and B are uncharged and attract each other.

**Answer: 4 (A and B are uncharged and attract each other)**

> **Explanation:** Van der Waals (dispersion) attraction between uncharged atoms decays as 1/r⁶, the steepest distance dependence among the listed interactions (salt bridge 1/r, dipole–dipole 1/r³). Cross-confirmed by Let's Talk Academy solution for this PYQ.

### Question 22

Which statement best describes the pKa of amino groups in proteins?

1. pKa of α-amino group is higher than the pKa of ε-amino group.
2. pKa of α-amino group is lower than the pKa of ε-amino group.
3. pKa of α-amino group is same as the pKa of ε-amino group.
4. pKa of α-amino group is higher than the pKa of guanidine side chain of arginine.

**Answer: 2 (pKa of α-amino group is lower than the pKa of ε-amino group)**

> **Explanation:** The α-amino group has pKa ≈ 8–9 whereas the ε-amino group of lysine has pKa ≈ 10.5, so α-amino is lower. The guanidinium group of arginine (pKa ≈ 12.5) is higher than α-amino, making option 4 false.

### Question 23

What is the effect of 2, 4-dinitrophenol on mitochondria?

1. Blocks ATP synthesis without inhibiting electron transport by dissipating the proton gradient.
2. Blocks electron transport and ATP synthesis by inhibiting ATP-ADP exchange across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
3. Blocks electron transport and proton pumping at complexes I, II and III.
4. Interacts directly with ATP synthase and inhibits its activity.

**Answer: 1 (Blocks ATP synthesis without inhibiting electron transport by dissipating the proton gradient)**

> **Explanation:** 2,4-DNP is an uncoupler — a lipid-soluble protonophore that carries protons across the inner membrane, collapsing the proton-motive force. Electron transport continues (even accelerates) but ATP synthesis stops.

### Question 24

A protein has 30% alanine. If all the alanines are replaced by glycines,

1. helical content will increase.
2. β-sheet content will increase.
3. there will be no change in conformation.
4. the alanine-substituted protein will be less structured than the parent protein.

**Answer: 4 (the alanine-substituted protein will be less structured than the parent protein)**

> **Explanation:** Alanine is a strong helix former; glycine is the strongest helix/β-sheet breaker (extreme conformational flexibility). Replacing alanine with glycine destabilises regular secondary structure, giving a less structured protein.

### Question 30

σ-subunit of E. coli RNA polymerase DOES NOT

1. initiate transcription and fall off during elongation.
2. increase affinity of the core enzyme to the promoter.
3. binds to DNA, independent of the core enzyme.
4. ensures specificity of transcription by interacting with the core enzyme.

**Answer: 1 (initiate transcription and fall off during elongation)**

> **Explanation:** σ confers promoter recognition/specificity, raises core affinity for the promoter, and free σ can bind promoter DNA on its own. Catalysis of the first phosphodiester bonds is performed by the core (β) enzyme, not by σ — σ merely helps form the open complex and is released after promoter clearance. So "initiate transcription" is not a σ function.

### Question 34

Which of the following is NOT associated with insulin action?

**Answer: 3 (Enhanced lipolysis in adipose tissue)**

### Question 48

The size of red blood cells (RBC) in venous blood is greater than that of arterial blood. This increased size of red blood cell in the venous blood is due to

1. the increased permeability of red blood cell (RBC) membrane.
2. the decreased osmotic pressure in plasma.
3. the increased osmotic pressure in RBC.
4. the dissociation of cytoskeletal proteins in RBC.

**Answer: 3 (the increased osmotic pressure in RBC)**

> **Explanation:** In venous blood CO₂ enters the red cell and is hydrated to bicarbonate while Cl⁻ flows in (the chloride shift). The extra solutes inside the cell raise its osmotic pressure, water follows, and the cell swells — which is why venous red cells are the larger ones.

### Question 70

In the case of monoclonal antibody production by hybridoma technology, myeloma cells used lack the enzyme hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyl transferase (HGPRT) such that fused cells can only survive when selected on hypoxanthine-aminopterin-thymidine (HAT).
